# Research Principles

The goal is ethical memorability and professional fascination, not manipulative attention capture.

## Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Attractive interfaces are often perceived as easier to use. The portfolio should use aesthetics to signal order, credibility, and care.

Implementation:

- Refined spacing
- Clear hierarchy
- Premium typography
- Minimal clutter
- High-quality interaction feedback

## Motion With Purpose

Motion should be brief, subtle, communicative, and useful for feedback, state changes, and navigation metaphors.

Implementation:

- Confirm actions.
- Show what changed.
- Preserve spatial context.
- Avoid constant ambient distraction.

## Peak-End Rule

People remember the peak and ending of an experience.

Implementation:

- Peak: cinematic identity reveal or Proof Graph.
- Ending: polished contact/resume CTA that feels like a closing title card.

## Von Restorff Effect

Distinctive items are more memorable.

Implementation:

- Gold Thread is the distinctive site-wide cue.
- Highlight only the strongest proof points.
- Avoid making every element loud.

## Serial Position Effect

People tend to remember the beginning and end.

Implementation:

- Begin with hybrid identity and credibility.
- End with resume, contact, and proof links.

## Doherty Threshold

Fast feedback keeps users engaged.

Implementation:

- Hover/tap feedback should feel immediate.
- Larger state changes should stay smooth and quick.
- Scroll-driven scenes can be slower because the user controls them.

## Shareability

Using Jonah Berger's STEPPS as inspiration:

- Social currency: make the site impressive enough to share.
- Triggers: Gold Thread and NK mark create recall.
- Emotion: create polish, momentum, and discovery.
- Public: make credentials and profiles verifiable.
- Practical value: visitors quickly find what they need.
- Stories: projects follow problem -> system -> impact.
